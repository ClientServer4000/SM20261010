# Beginner's Guide to the Indian Stock Market

Welcome to the Indian Stock Market! This guide expands on your notes, explaining fundamental concepts, trading types, order types, options trading, and technical analysis in clear, easy-to-understand language.

## 1. Market Timings & Days

- **Market Opening Time:** 9:15 AM IST
- **Market Closing Time:** 3:30 PM IST
- **Operating Days:** Monday to Friday

## 5. Delivery vs. Intraday Trading

When buying a share, you must choose between **Delivery** or **Intraday**.

```
                           BUYING SHARES
                                 │
                 ┌───────────────┴───────────────┐
                 ▼                               ▼
             DELIVERY                        INTRADAY
    (Hold for days/months/years)     (Buy & sell on the same day)
```

### A. Delivery Trading
- **What it means:** You buy shares and hold them for as long as you want (days, months, or years). Shares are transferred to your **Demat account**.
- **Risk Level:** Lower risk compared to intraday.
- **Leverage/Margin:** No extra margin; you pay 100% of the share price.

### B. Intraday Trading
- **What it means:** You must buy and sell (or sell and buy) the shares on the **same day** before the market closes (usually by 3:15 PM). If you don't close your trade, the broker automatically closes it for you.
- **5X Margin (Leverage):** If you have ₹1,000, your broker allows you to trade shares worth up to ₹5,000.
  - *Example:* A share costs ₹100. With ₹1,000, you can buy 10 shares in Delivery, but 50 shares in Intraday using 5X margin.

#### Pros & Cons of Intraday

| Pros | Cons |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Higher Profit Potential:** 5X margin increases your buying power. | **Higher Risk:** Losses are magnified by 5X just as profits are. |
| **Daily Liquidity:** Money is freed up at the end of every day. | **High Stress:** Requires constant monitoring during market hours. |
| **Profit from Falling Markets:** You can sell first and buy back later (Short Selling). | **Auto Square-off Charges:** Penalty or fees if the broker closes your trade at 3:15 PM. |

## 6. Order Types: Market Order vs. Limit Order

When placing an order on Angel One, you choose how your price is set:

1. **Market Order:**
   - **How it works:** You buy or sell immediately at whatever price is currently available in the market.
   - **Best for:** Fast execution when you want to enter/exit a trade right away.
   - **Example:** Buying Tata Motors at whatever price it is currently trading (e.g., ₹950.25).

2. **Limit Order:**
   - **How it works:** You set your preferred price. The order executes *only* if the stock price reaches your set target.
   - **Best for:** Getting a specific entry or exit price.
   - **Example:** Tata Motors is trading at ₹950, but you set a Limit Buy Order at ₹940. It will buy only if the price drops to ₹940.

## 7. Introduction to Options Trading

Options trading is an advanced type of trading where you do **not** buy actual stock shares. Instead, you buy derivative contracts.

```
                   OPTIONS TRADING
                          │
         ┌────────────────┴────────────────┐
         ▼                                 ▼
     CALL (CE)                         PUT (PE)
(Buy when market goes UP)         (Buy when market goes DOWN)
```

### Key Concepts

- **What you buy:** You buy **Premiums** (contract tokens), not full share values.
- **High Volatility:** Prices move extremely fast (up or down within seconds or minutes).
- **High Risk:** You can lose 100% of the money you invest in an option trade if the market moves against you or if time runs out (due to option expiry).

### Call (CE) vs. Put (PE)

- **CALL (CE):** You buy a Call Option when you expect the index/stock price to **GO UP** (Bullish view).
- **PUT (PE):** You buy a Put Option when you expect the index/stock price to **GO DOWN** (Bearish view).

### Options Trading in NIFTY 50 (For Beginners)
- Beginners are advised to practice with index options like **NIFTY 50** before individual stocks because NIFTY 50 is relatively less susceptible to manipulation than individual small-cap shares.
- **Important Warning:** Options trading carries significant risk. Beginners should practice with virtual trading tools or paper trading before risking real capital.

## 8. Technical Analysis & Candlestick Basics

Technical analysis involves reading price charts to predict future price moves.

### Candlestick Basics
Charts use visual "candles" to show price movements over a specific timeframe (e.g., 5-minute candle, 1-day candle).

```
   GREEN CANDLE (Bullish)            RED CANDLE (Bearish)
   
        High Price                       High Price
            │                                │
     ┌─────────────┐  Close           ┌─────────────┐  Open
     │             │                  │             │
     │  Body (UP)  │                  │ Body (DOWN) │
     │             │                  │             │
     └─────────────┘  Open            └─────────────┘  Close
            │                                │
        Low Price                        Low Price
```

- **Green Candle:** The price closed higher than it opened (Upward movement).
- **Red Candle:** The price closed lower than it opened (Downward movement).

## 9. Key Market Conditions & Trends

### 1. Downtrend
- **Meaning:** The overall direction of the market or stock is moving lower over time.
- **Pattern:** The chart makes "Lower Highs" and "Lower Lows."
- **Action:** Caution for long-term investors; opportunity for short-sellers or Put buyers.

### 2. Uptrend
- **Meaning:** The overall direction of the market or stock is moving higher over time.
- **Pattern:** The chart makes "Higher Highs" and "Higher Lows."
- **Action:** Favorable for buying shares or Call options.

### 3. Resistance & Support

```
   Resistance Line ───────────────────────────────── (Ceiling: Price bounces down)
                           /\          /\
                          /  \        /  \
                         /    \      /    \
                        /      \    /      \
   Support Line ──────────────────────────────────── (Floor: Price bounces up)
```

- **Support:** A price level below current market value where buying interest is strong enough to prevent the price from falling further. Think of it as a **floor**.
- **Resistance:** A price level above current market value where selling pressure is strong enough to prevent the price from rising further. Think of it as a **ceiling**.
